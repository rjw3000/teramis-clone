Define Your CUI Boundary From the CUI You Actually Have

CUI scoping should begin with evidence of where Controlled Unclassified Information enters, resides, moves, and appears across the environment.

Find the data before you define the boundary.

THE FOUNDATION OF DEFENSIBLE CUI SCOPING

Your CUI Boundary Is Only as Accurate as the Data Behind It

Most organizations begin CUI planning with interviews, asset inventories, documentation, and assumptions about where CUI should be located. Those sources are useful, but they do not prove where CUI actually exists.

CUI may be scattered across Microsoft 365, file shares, email, endpoints, CAD repositories, scanned documents, archives, engineering folders, and legacy systems.

Over-scoping increases licensing, infrastructure, assessment, consulting, and security-control costs. Under-scoping creates security gaps, assessment surprises, rework, and risk tied to inaccurate compliance representations.

Before you build the boundary, buy the technology, or prepare for assessment, find the CUI you actually have.

START WITH THE ACTUAL DATA ENVIRONMENT

Do Not Build a CUI Program Around Assumptions

Why CUI Scoping Comes First

Many organizations begin NIST SP 800-171 work by selecting a secure platform, purchasing licenses, drafting an SSP, implementing controls, or designing an enclave.

Those decisions may be premature if the organization has not established where CUI actually exists.

Interviews, questionnaires, policies, diagrams, and asset inventories can describe the intended environment. They may not reveal CUI that has been copied into local folders, sent through email, stored in unexpected SharePoint sites, downloaded to endpoints, included in engineering repositories, embedded in PDFs or scanned documents, retained in archives, shared through unexpected workflows, or stored in legacy and distributed systems.

Teramis is built around a simple principle: CUI scope should reflect the actual data environment, not only documentation or institutional memory.

Over-Scoping and Under-Scoping Create Different Risks

An Inaccurate Boundary Can Increase Cost or Leave Important Gaps

Scope decisions shape licensing, infrastructure, controls, documentation, assessment preparation, and ongoing monitoring. The goal is not simply to make the boundary smaller. It is to make the boundary more accurate.

Scope Risk 01

Over-Scoped Environment

An organization may include more systems, users, devices, applications, and repositories than necessary because it does not know where CUI actually resides.

  • • Secure-platform licensing
  • • Infrastructure requirements
  • • Control implementation
  • • Documentation work
  • • Evidence collection
  • • Consulting effort
  • • Internal administration
  • • Future assessment complexity

Scope Risk 02

Under-Scoped Environment

An organization may exclude systems or repositories that contain CUI because those locations were not identified during planning.

  • • Incomplete asset inventories
  • • Unsupported SSP assumptions
  • • CUI outside the documented boundary
  • • Missed data flows
  • • Self-attestation gaps
  • • Rework after later discovery
  • • Uncertainty around self-assessment conclusions
  • • Greater risk when customers or government reviewers request evidence

Evidence-Based Scope

Better-Scoped Environment

A better-scoped environment is based on evidence showing where CUI is present, where it is not, and where the current boundary needs further review.

  • • Which systems belong inside the boundary
  • • Which users require access
