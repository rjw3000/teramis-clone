Answer One Question Factually: Was CUI in the Affected Systems?

Determine Whether CUI Was Present After an Incident

After a security incident, the response team, counsel, and leadership need to know what data was in the environment that was touched. For organizations handling Controlled Unclassified Information, that question carries contractual and regulatory weight.

Teramis examines approved portions of affected systems and repositories to help determine whether CUI may have been present, and produces a factual record of what was found.

Teramis supports factual discovery. Your counsel and response team make the determinations.

The cost of an assumption

Why Accurate CUI Discovery Matters After a Security Incident

When an organization cannot establish what data lived in an affected system, it is forced to reason from assumption. Both directions carry consequences.

Overstatement

Assuming CUI Was Present When It Was Not

This can trigger notifications, customer escalations, contractual disclosures, and remediation work that the facts would not have required.

Understatement

Assuming CUI Was Not Present When It Was

This can leave obligations unmet and create more serious exposure later, particularly if the facts surface during a subsequent review.

Factual data-location evidence narrows the range the response team is reasoning within.

A narrow, defined role

Data-Location Evidence for the People Making the Decisions

Working within the systems and repositories your organization and counsel authorize for examination, Teramis can help establish the relevant facts about the affected environment.

Whether candidate CUI was present in the affected environment

Which repositories and locations contained it

File-level detail on what was found

Whether findings extend beyond the initially suspected scope

What the environment looks like after response activity

Teramis provides a factual input to an incident process led by others. It does not perform an incident investigation.

Sequence and authorization

Discovery Follows Containment and Legal Direction

Containment and Forensics Lead

Your incident response team and forensic providers handle containment, root cause, and evidence preservation. Teramis does not participate in that work.

Counsel Defines the Scope of Examination

Legal counsel and your organization determine which systems and repositories may be examined and under what conditions.

Discovery Runs Against the Authorized Scope

Teramis examines only what has been authorized.

Findings Go to the Decision-Makers

Results are provided to the teams responsible for determining impact, obligations, and reporting.

Post-Response Verification

After remediation activity, re-scanning can confirm the current state of the environment.

A factual record

Documentation Your Counsel and Response Team Can Work From

Provide the people responsible for legal, contractual, and response decisions with a clearer record of the examined environment.

Organizations with an existing Teramis baseline are in a materially better position. The affected environment can be compared against a known prior state rather than assessed cold.

Discovery Evidence, Not Incident Determinations

Teramis does not:

  • Perform incident response, containment, or digital forensics
  • Determine whether a breach or reportable event occurred
  • Make legal, regulatory, reporting, or notification determinations
  • Establish whether data was accessed, exfiltrated, or viewed
